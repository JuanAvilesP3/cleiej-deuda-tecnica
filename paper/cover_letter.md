Dear Editors-in-Chief and Editorial Board of *CLEI Electronic Journal*,

We are pleased to submit our original research manuscript titled **"Technical Debt in Latin American Academic Software: A Matched-Sample Comparison with Open-Source Controls"** for consideration as a regular research article in *CLEI Electronic Journal*. Our study directly aligns with the core scope of CLEIej, which aims to provide high visibility to empirical computer science and software engineering research originating in Latin America. Specifically, this work addresses a long-standing assumption in computing education across the region: whether software produced in university courses and capstone projects carries higher technical debt than non-academic software.

In this paper, we report a quantitative, matched-sample empirical study analyzing **549 GitHub repositories** across **13 Latin American countries** (274 academic repositories matched by programming language and size band against 275 non-academic control repositories). Contrary to widespread belief, our non-parametric hypothesis tests reveal that academic repositories exhibit *significantly lower* cyclomatic complexity than control repositories (median 2.16 vs. 3.12, Cliff's $\delta = -0.37$, $p < 10^{-11}$), a finding that holds consistently across Java, Python, and JavaScript subsets. Conversely, academic Java code presents deeper inheritance hierarchies (DIT, $\delta = +0.29$, $p = 0.003$) and a higher incidence of self-admitted technical debt comments (SATD, $\delta = +0.19$, $p < 0.001$). An independent corroboration using SonarQube Community on a 415-repository subset confirms the direction and magnitude of these structural findings, providing actionable empirical evidence on the nature of student and hobbyist software engineering in Latin America.

We confirm that this manuscript represents original work, has not been published previously, and is not currently under consideration for publication in any other journal or conference proceedings.
